why roon exists

You are taking more photographs than any generation in history — and losing more memories.

The camera roll was supposed to solve this. It didn't. It gave us volume without meaning: thousands of images nobody will ever open, filed under a date that tells you nothing about what actually happened that day.

Google Photos and iCloud keep your files. That's storage, and they're good at it. Roon is not storage. Rooney is the storyteller — the part nobody built. Rooney turns your photos, video, and words into written chapters that remember why a moment mattered.

threads rooney is following

Your photos aren’t a pile. They’re full of threads.

A thread is simple: one person, place, or day your family keeps coming back to — a garden, a dog, every birthday cake — gathered from every year it appears in your photos, and told in order.

Rooney assembles yours from your own media and words — and every count it ever shows you is a real count of your own moments.

She brings something back from every walk. Flowers, rocks, one time a snail. Forty-one walks, and the pattern never breaks.

more than photographs

A memory is never just a photograph.

It's the sound of her voice at four. The thing you scribbled at 2am because you didn't want to forget it. The video nobody meant to take. Roon brings all of it together — and Rooney combines it into chapters, in whatever form it arrived.

where the name came from

Roon is named after my daughter, Rooney.

At four, she drew the dinosaur that is our logo. We never redrew it. I have it tattooed on me.

Rooney has a rare autoimmune disease, and no cure. She also lives more in a week than most people manage in a year.

Watching her taught me how fast this goes — and that the days you’d give anything to have back are almost never the ones you thought to photograph. Roon exists to give those days back.
