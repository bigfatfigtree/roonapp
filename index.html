The problem

You take more photos than any generation in history — and almost none of them are ever seen again.

They pile up by the thousand. Birthdays, ordinary Tuesdays, your mother’s voice in an old voicemail. The pile is where they stay — not because they don’t matter, but because nobody has time to make them into something.

Meet Rooney

The storyteller inside Roon.

Rooney is the storyteller who lives inside Roon — she reads your family’s photos and writes the story back to you. Chapters, not albums. And when you want a memory, you just ask her.

Where the name came from

Roon is named after my daughter, Rooney.

At four, she drew the dinosaur that is our logo. We never redrew it. I have it tattooed on me.

Rooney has a rare autoimmune disease, and no cure. She also lives more in a week than most people manage in a year.

Watching her taught me how fast this goes — and that the days you’d give anything to have back are almost never the ones you thought to photograph. Roon exists to give those days back.
